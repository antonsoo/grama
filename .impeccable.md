## Design Context

### Users

English-speaking learners exploring ancient music and beginning Sanskrit. They
need to distinguish the text's count instructions from modern pitch assumptions,
and to connect an operated listening experiment to its printed evidence.

### Brand Personality

Curious, exact, tactile. The user's direction is distinctive educational work
with thematic soul and real functionality, avoiding generic showcase templates.

### Aesthetic Direction

Extend the existing instrument bench: restrained blue rules, open white space,
large named listening keys, plum for the retuned note. Keep the bench's local
fonts. Native controls, readable phones, keyboard operation, no ornamental
waveforms, imitation ancient materials, badges or decorative dashboards.

### Design Principles

- Give learners a question they can answer by operating the instrument.
- Give color a stable meaning and keep explanations next to the relevant action.
- Separate concealed listening from revealed numerical evidence.
- Preserve unsuccessful attempts as honestly as successful ones.
- Make source reading and portable saved work part of the experiment.

Context: the current user's English-speaking audience, ancient music/language
brief and autonomous refinement instruction; existing docs/design.md supplies
the established bench treatment. No additional preferences were solicited while
the owner sleeps.
